# Patterns — Settings

### Desktop settings master-detail

A menu-style tab whose sub-screens are settings forms (not book detail) gets its own *feature-internal* master–detail on desktop (`jvmMain`), analogous to Library's shelf-sidebar (§3.6's large-window list/grid rule): a category **source-list** sidebar (`hoverHighlight` + `pointerHandCursor` rows, `secondaryContainer` on the selected row) beside a detail pane that swaps **inline** — selecting a category never pushes. Settings is the reference: *Account → Your profile* still pushes full-screen (Profile is its own surface), while *Appearance* and *Library tabs* swap the pane, each scrolling under a `DesktopVerticalScrollbar`, with the Library-tabs save bar docked at the pane's bottom. The sub-screen bodies are shared composables (mobile wraps them in a pushed `TopBar` page; desktop hosts them in the pane and in a standalone back-bar fallback). The hosted sub-screen's model is acquired in the tab's `Content` (Voyager's `koinScreenModel` is a `Screen` extension and resolves only in `commonMain`), gated by a per-feature `expect val` so mobile doesn't create it — mirroring `wideGridTabsUseDetailPane`. **Desktop Library and Explore opt out of the two-pane** (each has a bespoke full-width layout — Library's shelf-sidebar + adaptive grid, Explore's discovery grids — where a side detail pane only starves the grid): on the desktop (`jvmMain`) target neither wide-grid tab is detail-capable, so a tapped book pushes the detail full-screen — while mobile/tablet keep their two-pane. Reading, a detail-style reading list, keeps the two-pane on every platform. The opt-out is the platform seam `wideGridTabsUseDetailPane` (Library and Explore are both gated on it); `BookDetailPresenter` fills the pane only when the current tab is detail-capable on an expanded window, and pushes otherwise.

### Theme picker (preview tiles)

The Appearance body **opens** with the theme choice, on every platform — it is the one control that repaints the whole app, so it leads even on desktop, ahead of "Display scale". Anatomy: an `EditorialSectionHeader` (**Theme** / "The coat it wears"), then a `Row` of one **Theme preview tile** (components.md) per `ThemeMode` — Light, Dark, System, in that order, the deferring option last — then a single italic-Fraunces gloss line naming what System means ("System follows your device's own light and dark setting."), rather than a caption under each tile. Tiles are weighted but **capped at 128dp** (`weight(1f, fill = false)` + `widthIn(max =)`): a tile is a picture, not a text block, so it stops growing once it is big enough to read instead of stretching into three posters across a desktop pane. Dynamic colour belongs to the **spine-colour picker** below (see "Spine-colour picker") — the theme tiles still re-render in the wallpaper palette the instant it goes on, but the choice it competes with there is the palette, not the brightness. Picking a mode writes straight through (no draft, no confirm) and the whole app repaints under the reader's finger — that immediacy is the point of previewing the choice in the first place. **The chosen mode is the app's answer to "is it dark", not the device's**, so a surface deciding an alpha or an ink by brightness reads `LocalDarkTheme` — never `isSystemInDarkTheme()`, which goes wrong the moment a reader forces the opposite of their device — and the platform chrome Compose does not paint (Android's system-bar glyph contrast, iOS's status bar) is re-pointed at the resolved mode through the shell's `ApplyPlatformThemeAppearance` seam.

### Spine-colour picker (the featured personalisation)

Directly beneath the theme picker sits the app's answer to Material You in its own register: an `EditorialSectionHeader` (**Spine colour** / "The paper and the ink"), then one **Colour palette preview tile** (components.md) per `ColorPalette` (§2.1), each mapped to its `SpinePalette` token on the way in — five curated looks, paper and accents together, the house palette first — then a single italic-Fraunces gloss line. Two rules give it its shape. **The tiles are measured, not weighted:** the five wrap as a 3 + 2 grid (`FlowRow`, `maxItemsInEachRow = 3`) inside a `BoxWithConstraints` that hands every tile the same width — a third of the row, capped at the same 128dp the theme tiles use — because with `weight` the wrapped second row's two tiles would each claim half the width and end up visibly larger than the three above them. **The gloss line names the *selected* palette** (`SpinePalette.gloss`, reached through `toSpinePalette()` — "Deep sea teal, with warm sand at the edge") rather than repeating one fixed sentence; it is the only place this picker speaks in words, so it says something that changes as the reader moves along the row. **Dynamic colour is this section's tail** (one hairline, then the standard toggle row, still gated on `supportsDynamicColor()` — so on iOS and desktop the section is the tiles alone) because it is the alternative *scheme source*, not a peer of "floating bottom bar": while it is on the gloss line swaps to say so, the tiles keep showing their own palettes, and **tapping any tile switches dynamic colour off** (`SetColorPaletteUseCase` writes both) — a picker whose tiles did nothing while dynamic colour was on would be a row of dead controls. Like the theme picker it writes straight through, with no draft and no confirm.

### Appearance settings rows (flat, hairline-separated)

The Appearance settings body (`AppearanceSettingsContent`, shared by the mobile page and the desktop Settings master–detail pane) is built from **flat rows sitting directly on the page background, split by `outlineVariant` hairlines** — not boxed `surfaceContainer` cards. It opens with an italic-Fraunces intro line, then rations the accent-bar ceremony to **four** `EditorialSectionHeader`s — **Theme** ("The coat it wears") and **Spine colour** ("The paper and the ink"), both covered above, then **Display** ("The look") and **Date notation** ("How dates read") — rather than one per control. Two row primitives compose it: a **toggle row** (label in `titleMedium`/`onSurface` over a one-line italic-Fraunces gloss in `onSurfaceVariant`, with a trailing M3 `Switch` — the group's only accent element; no per-row bar, icon tile, or "On/Off" caption) collects the peer switches (floating bar, shelf swipe, reading streak) under the single Display header — and carries dynamic colour under the Spine colour header above — that label-over-gloss row anatomy is the app's canonical *captioned* binary control and travels beyond Settings — including onto Profile's "Who you read" section (see "Profile screen", explore-profile.md) for its "Hide untagged authors" switch — wherever a switch needs a sentence to explain what it means; a switch whose label already says everything (the Arrange sheet's "Show titles & authors") stays a bare label + `Switch` row instead. Each surface owns its own copy of the composable rather than reaching across the feature boundary; and a **selectable row** (label coloured `primary` when active, an optional example subtitle beneath, and a trailing `primary` `SoftcoverIcon.Check` on the active row — **no radio circle / `SelectionIndicator`, no card**) drives the single-choice sections. The Date-notation selectable rows carry each format's own result as the example — today's date rendered through that `DateStyle` (`DateStyle.format`, held in `SettingsScreenUiState.dateStyleExamples`), so the choice explains itself.

### Desktop display scale

The same Appearance body exposes a **desktop-only** "Display scale" section, gated by `showUiScaleControl` (`true` on both desktop entry points — the standalone Appearance page and the Settings master–detail pane; `false` on mobile, where the OS handles DPI). It is a discrete `UiScale` selector (100/125/150/175/200%) rendered with the **same flat selectable-row** anatomy as Date notation (label + trailing active check; no example subtitle), placed directly after the theme and spine-colour pickers since it is the appearance control that matters next on desktop. The chosen `UiScale.factor` scales `LocalDensity` at the desktop composition root, applying immediately app-wide.

### About screen body

`AboutContent` (shared `commonMain`, used by the mobile pushed About page, the desktop standalone About fallback, and the desktop Settings master–detail pane's `About` category) opens with an unheaded italic intro line — the page title itself is chrome the caller already renders (a top bar on mobile, a static header on desktop) — then three `EditorialSectionHeader` groups: **Credits** (the Hardcover.app link, plus the "independent, open-source… not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by" disclaimer beneath it, in prose, not as a second row), **Source** (the GitHub repository link), and **Contact** (report-an-issue, the developer's Discord username as plain selectable text — see "About link/username row", components/navigation.md, for why it isn't a tap-to-copy action — and the Hardcover Discord invite as a link), and closes unconditionally with `VersionFooter`. About is the app's **one and only** place the version shows — neither the mobile Settings menu nor the desktop sidebar carries its own version line (nor, on the desktop master–detail surface, do the sidebar and pane show it at once) — so `AboutContent` renders it itself, unconditionally, rather than taking a per-host toggle. OSS license text is explicitly out of scope for this surface. The screen is deliberately structured to take a **Changelog** and a **Roadmap** row later without restructuring — do not pre-add disabled rows for those before the features exist.

### Library tabs reorderable row

The Library-tabs list under Settings → Library tabs: one flat, borderless `Column` of rows (radius 12dp, no card, no hairline dividers between rows), matching the flattened register of the rest of Settings — a list mixing a fixed leading row, drag-to-reorder, and a per-row show/hide affordance reads lightest this way. The screen opens once with the canonical `EditorialSectionHeader`, then a bar-less group eyebrow + italic subhead (the "a list" legend's dot inlined mid-sentence via `InlineTextContent`, not a separate leading glyph) — no second accent bar, since that would be group-level ceremony the header above already spent. **Row anatomy:** a leading 26dp grip/pin column — a `primary`-while-dragging `ic_drag_handle` glyph (`onSurfaceVariant` idle) for a reorderable entry, or a demoted (`onSurfaceVariant` @ 0.5 alpha) pin glyph for the one fixed front row that can neither move nor be moved past (the built-in "All" shelf) — then the name (Inter, roman, a leading 6dp `primary` dot for a custom list, none for a built-in shelf) over an italic Fraunces tabular count line ("N titles", swapped for "Hidden from tabs" while the row is hidden), and a trailing 38dp circular eye toggle (filled `primary` eye when shown, outline struck-eye when hidden) — the row's only show/hide affordance, present only on a row that can be hidden at all (the fixed "All" row and an always-visible status such as Currently Reading carry none). A hidden row drops to 52% opacity. Because no single tap opens a row — only the grip drags and the eye toggles — the row's "press wash" (`animateColorAsState` to `surfaceContainer`, gated by `playDecorativeMotion()`) reflects the row's own drag state rather than a tap, washing only while its grip is picked up. **Drag mechanics:** pressing the handle picks the row up immediately (no long-press; the handle *is* the affordance) — fire `Haptics.lift()` (§2.5), tint the handle `primary`, and translate the row vertically with the drag via `graphicsLayer { translationY }`; the other rows stay put during the drag (no intermediate-slot snapping) and only shift once the drop target is computed by walking neighbouring row heights past their midpoint, firing `Haptics.drop()` on release. Gesture wiring is `Modifier.draggable(orientation = Vertical, startDragImmediately = true)`, applied only to a reorderable row's grip column, so it composes with a vertically-scrolling parent without fighting it; the fixed front row carries no draggable modifier at all and the drop-target computation is clamped so nothing can land above it. A full-width **New list** foot action closes the list: a 34dp `primaryContainer` circular plus tile beside a `primary` label, opening the list-creation flow. Long-press *outside* the row (e.g. on a tab pill in the Library strip) is the canonical shortcut into this screen — fire `Haptics.threshold()` and route here rather than spawning an inline reorder mode. Reach for this anatomy for any other settings-style list that mixes a fixed leading row, drag-to-reorder, and a per-row show/hide toggle.
