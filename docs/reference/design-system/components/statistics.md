# Components — Statistics

### Editorial figure / chart

The family of small, hand-drawn (feature-local, Profile) (not library-backed) charts that render a distribution or history inline in the editorial voice, in Profile's "reading life" section (`feature/profile`'s `ProfileShelf.kt`). The first four share one colour rule — **monochrome `primary`, never a categorical palette** — because a multi-colour chart would compete with the app's single-accent rule (§2.1); shape (bar height, segment width, fill length) carries the data, alpha carries emphasis. The fifth (the demographic breakdown bar) is a deliberate, narrow exception to that rule. This is the flat-bar exception the "wavy progress, always" rule (§2.5/§6) doesn't cover: those bars are **chrome** (a determinate loading/mutation state), these are **data** — a histogram or proportion bar has no "in progress" reading, so it stays a flat `RoundedCornerShape`-clipped `Box`, never the wavy indicator.

**Picking between the two bar families is a data question, not a taste one.** A *stack* (one bar cut into segments) asserts that its parts partition a whole; *ranked share bars* (one track per category) assert nothing about the parts' relationship. Use the stack only when the categories are mutually exclusive and exhaust the population — every author is exactly one gender, every author is Yes/No/Unknown on BIPOC. Use ranked share bars the moment an item can fall into several categories at once, because then there is no whole to divide: shares can each approach 100% and sum well past it, and a stack drawn over them is a false claim. Reach for these five shapes before hand-rolling a new one:

- **Peak-annotated bar column** — a `Row` of per-category columns (year, in Profile's case), each a fixed-width `primary` bar sized `minHeight + (value / max) * rangeHeight`, full alpha (1f) on the peak column and ~0.82 alpha on every other column, with the peak value numeral floating above only its own bar and a tabular category label beneath every bar. A hairline `outlineVariant` baseline closes the chart.
- **Ranked share bars** — one row per category, each a full-width `RoundedCornerShape`-clipped track (`onSurfaceVariant` at ~14% alpha) carrying a `primary` fill sized by `fillMaxWidth(fraction)`, over a header row of name (weighted, single-line, ellipsised) and tabular percentage. **The track is always the full 100%**, never normalised to the leading category, so a bar's length is legible on its own rather than only against its neighbours. Rank is carried by the same five stepping alphas the stack below uses (100/82/64/48/34%), so two categories within a point of each other still read as first and second. The fill is floored at ~1.5% so a small real share still draws, and capped at the track; unlike a stack, that floor costs no neighbour anything, because each row is its own 100%. Used by Profile's "The genres you read most" (top five genres, each as a share of the reader's genre-tagged books). **This is the shape for overlapping categories** — a book carries several genres, so the shares are independent, each can reach 100% on its own, and they routinely total well past it. Never add a total, an "everything else" remainder, or a stacked rendering to a set drawn this way. The section closes with a caption naming the denominator in words (the count of books carrying any genre tag), because a share-of-books number is unreadable until the reader knows which books were counted; untagged books are outside that denominator entirely.
- **Ranked proportion stack** — a single full-width `Row` of segments, each `weight`ed to its slice's fraction and filled `primary` at one of the five stepping alphas (100/82/64/48/34%) in rank order — the top slice reads solid, each subsequent slice fades a step, and the alphas repeat as the swatch colour in the legend rows beneath (swatch + name + tabular percentage, hairline-separated). **Only for mutually-exclusive categories that exhaust the population**, since the shape asserts a partition. Profile's gender breakdown is the canonical use: up to three ranked slices (Women/Men/Other) plus `Unknown` as a real fourth slice pinned last, drawn in the muted non-`primary` treatment the demographic bar below uses for its own Unknown segment. That Unknown slice disappears — and the remaining three renormalise over the gender-tagged authors alone — while the reader has the section's "hide untagged authors" switch on (see the demographic breakdown bar below). Overlapping data belongs in the ranked share bars above, not this stack.
- **Half-star ratings histogram** — nine bars at half-star resolution; whole-star bars render at full width and 100% alpha, half-star bars at roughly two-thirds that width and ~55% alpha, so the eye reads the whole-star bars as the primary series and the halves as in-between texture. X-axis labels appear only under the whole-star bars.
- **Demographic breakdown bar** — a fixed **three**-segment Yes/No/Unknown bar, never ranked or resized (unlike the ranked proportion stack above, which drops to fewer segments when there's less data). Yes fills `primary` at full alpha, No `primary` at ~55% alpha; **Unknown is the one deliberate break from this family's monochrome-`primary` rule** — it fills `onSurfaceVariant` at ~22% alpha, a muted non-`primary` neutral, so an untagged share reads as "we don't know" rather than as a third positive category sitting alongside Yes/No. Same hairline-separated swatch + name + tabular-percentage legend as the stacks above, plus a count caption ("Yes 2 · No 5 · Unknown 23 · of 30 authors"). Used by Profile's "Who you read" BIPOC and LGBTQ+ breakdowns, each scoped by default to **every** distinct tagged author — not just the ones with that attribute known — so a large untagged population is always visible rather than folded into a share-of-known percentage that would otherwise skew high. **A zero bucket draws nothing**: a segment with no authors in it is omitted rather than given the 1%-floor sliver the bar reserves for tiny *real* shares, and the Unknown legend row renders only when there is an untagged share to report (Yes/No are fixed categories and stay at 0%). An entirely empty breakdown keeps its eyebrow and drops to the caption alone rather than drawing a bar of nothing.
  - **Hide untagged authors (the section's one control).** The default reading above is the honest one, but it makes the tagged shares unreadable when most authors are untagged, so "Who you read" carries a single switch — the [Appearance-settings toggle-row anatomy](../patterns/settings.md#appearance-settings-rows-flat-hairline-separated) borrowed onto a stats section, label over an italic-Fraunces gloss, trailing M3 `Switch` — that flips every bar in the section to the share-of-tagged-authors reading. The exclusion is **per bar, not per author**: each bar renormalises over the authors tagged for *its own* attribute, so the three don't share a denominator and each caption must therefore name its own total ("of N tagged authors"). Every caption in the section restates its scope when the switch is on; the Unknown term drops out of the count caption entirely. The switch renders only when there is an untagged bucket to drop, is persisted (`:core:preferences`), and sits **outside** the section's empty-state branch so a reader whose authors are all untagged can always switch back rather than being stranded in an empty section.

All five are **stateless** (a plain `List<Int>` / `List<Double>` / ranked-slice list / three-count breakdown in, a `Row` or `Column` of `Box`es out) and currently **profile-local** — promote to a shared `core/designsystem` chart primitive only once a second real screen needs the same shape; until then a second consumer should copy the composable rather than reach across the feature boundary.
