# Design System — Patterns

## 5. Patterns

Recurring recipes that compose the primitives above.

- **Editorial section.** Accent bar (optional) → eyebrow → display headline → body (carousel, list, or block). The default way to introduce any region.
- **List–detail two-pane.** On expanded windows (§2.7) a root list surface (library, reading, explore) keeps its list/grid in a fixed-width leading pane and renders the selected book's detail in the trailing pane, backed by `TwoPaneScaffold` (§3.6). Selecting a book swaps the trailing pane **instantly** — the cover-to-detail morph (§2.5) is a property of the compact full-screen push, not the pane swap, so the pane host suppresses it. Before a book is chosen the trailing pane shows a quiet editorial placeholder, never a blank surface. The pattern is **expanded-only**: at compact and medium widths the same tap pushes the detail full-screen as before — the shell's `BookDetailPresenter` (not the feature) owns the push-vs-pane decision, so a list screen stays unaware of which it gets. Tabs that don't open book detail (Settings) stay single-surface in the *shell* sense (they never take the book-detail trailing pane), and full-screen pushed surfaces (focus mode, scanner, full-screen cover) plus onboarding are exempt — they render outside the shell and stay full-bleed.

- **Desktop settings master–detail.** A menu-style tab whose sub-screens are settings forms (not book detail) gets its own *feature-internal* master–detail on desktop (`jvmMain`), analogous to Library's shelf-sidebar (§the list/grid rules): a category **source-list** sidebar (`hoverHighlight` + `pointerHandCursor` rows, `secondaryContainer` on the selected row) beside a detail pane that swaps **inline** — selecting a category never pushes. Settings is the reference: *Account → Your profile* still pushes full-screen (Profile is its own surface), while *Appearance* and *Library tabs* swap the pane, each scrolling under a `DesktopVerticalScrollbar`, with the Library-tabs save bar docked at the pane's bottom. The sub-screen bodies are shared composables (mobile wraps them in a pushed `SoftcoverTopBar` page; desktop hosts them in the pane and in a standalone back-bar fallback). The hosted sub-screen's model is acquired in the tab's `Content` (Voyager's `koinScreenModel` is a `Screen` extension and resolves only in `commonMain`), gated by a per-feature `expect val` so mobile doesn't create it — mirroring `wideGridTabsUseDetailPane`. **Desktop Library and Explore opt out of the two-pane** (each has a bespoke full-width layout — Library's shelf-sidebar + adaptive grid, Explore's discovery grids — where a side detail pane only starves the grid): on the desktop (`jvmMain`) target neither wide-grid tab is detail-capable, so a tapped book pushes the detail full-screen — while mobile/tablet keep their two-pane. Reading, a detail-style reading list, keeps the two-pane on every platform. The opt-out is the platform seam `wideGridTabsUseDetailPane` (Library and Explore are both gated on it); `BookDetailPresenter` fills the pane only when the current tab is detail-capable on an expanded window, and pushes otherwise.
- **Appearance settings rows (flat, hairline-separated).** The Appearance settings body (`AppearanceSettingsContent`, shared by the mobile page and the desktop Settings master–detail pane) is built from **flat rows sitting directly on the page background, split by `outlineVariant` hairlines** — *not* boxed `surfaceContainer` cards and *not* the (now-retired) `SettingsGroup` surface — see "Library tabs reorderable row" (§5) for its former sole consumer's replacement. It opens with an italic-Fraunces intro line, then rations the accent-bar ceremony to **two** `EditorialSectionHeader`s — **Display** ("The look") and **Date notation** ("How dates read") — rather than one per control. Two row primitives compose it: a **toggle row** (label in `titleMedium`/`onSurface` over a one-line italic-Fraunces gloss in `onSurfaceVariant`, with a trailing M3 `Switch` — the group's only accent element; no per-row bar, icon tile, or "On/Off" caption) collects the peer switches (dynamic colour, floating bar, reading streak) under the single Display header; and a **selectable row** (label coloured `primary` when active, an optional example subtitle beneath, and a trailing `primary` `SoftcoverIcon.Check` on the active row — **no radio circle / `SelectionIndicator`, no card**) drives the single-choice sections. The Date-notation selectable rows carry each format's own result as the example — today's date rendered through that `DateStyle` (`DateStyle.format`, held in `SettingsScreenUiState.dateStyleExamples`), so the choice explains itself.
- **Desktop display scale.** The same Appearance body exposes a **desktop-only** "Display scale" section, gated by `showUiScaleControl` (`true` on both desktop entry points — the standalone Appearance page and the Settings master–detail pane; `false` on mobile, where the OS handles DPI). It is a discrete `UiScale` selector (100/125/150/175/200%) rendered with the **same flat selectable-row** anatomy as Date notation (label + trailing active check; no example subtitle), placed first in the column since it is the most relevant appearance control on desktop. The chosen `UiScale.factor` scales `LocalDensity` at the desktop composition root, applying immediately app-wide.
- **Hero stat.** A single oversize number in `statHero` or `statLarge`, an editorial suffix beneath in italic body, on-surface-variant. Used for "books read this year", "pages today", and progress sheets. The unit (e.g. "pages", "books") is named in the eyebrow above the number, never trailed next to it — pairing a unit suffix with the oversize numeral fights for horizontal space and breaks once the number grows. The number itself uses single-line autosize down from `statHero` so power-reader counts (six- or seven-digit page totals) still fit the card without reflowing the layout. The numeral is rendered via `AnimatedStatNumber` (§4) so changes tween in place — raw `Text` for a live-bound stat is a bug.
- **Editable hero stat.** Same as hero stat, but the number is editable — rendered through the shared `HeroStatNumberField` component (§4): a borderless `BasicTextField` styled with `statLarge`, cursor in primary colour, width computed from character count so the field does not jump as digits are typed. Pair it with an `EditorialSuffix` caption ("of 195 pages", "of the way through"). Used by the Update progress sheet and Focus Mode; do not hand-roll a boxed field for an editable hero number.
- **Editorial quote.** Oversized low-alpha `quoteGlyph` (tinted `onSurfaceVariant`) behind an italic headline-medium pull quote, with a primary-coloured byline beneath. Used to surface notes, reviews, or a featured passage. The same glyph treatment also serves as a decorative flourish in empty states (e.g. "no books currently reading"), where the quote text below is replaced by an italic encouragement headline and no byline. In empty states only, the glyph plays a slow infinite ±2° sway (~6.8s each direction, FastOutSlowIn) via `Modifier.quoteGlyphSway()` — a magazine pull-quote breathing, never a marquee. Do not apply the sway to populated quote cards (review cards, share cards) — content next to the glyph fights a moving mark for attention. Suppressed under reduced motion (§2.5 gate).
- **Spoiler reveal.** A review hides its spoilers at two independent granularities. The **whole-review gate** is for a review flagged as containing spoilers: the body is withheld behind a quiet "Show spoiler" text button (reveal state held transiently per review id) — used on community review cards. The **inline span reveal** is for individual spoiler runs *inside* an otherwise-visible review: the run is rendered as a solid `spoilerCover` block (text drawn transparent) and reveals in place when the reader taps that span (see `ReviewDocumentText`, §4). The two compose — a review can be gated as a whole *and* contain inline spoiler spans — but they are separate affordances; never collapse one into the other. In the editor, the same inline mark previews as the translucent `spoilerEditorHighlight` wash so the author keeps reading their words.
- **Verdict (combined rating + review).** A book's personal rating and personal review are **never** two separate surfaces — one half-star `StarRatingInput` plus one optional written review, edited together in one `VerdictSheet` (§4) and shown together in one `VerdictBlock` (§4) on the book page. This retires the earlier split pattern (a standalone "Your rating" row with an always-live `StarRatingInput`, beside a separate "Your review" card opening its own `ReviewEditorBottomSheet`) — rating and review now share one seam because they are answers to the same question ("what did you think?"), and a reader who only wants to leave a number no longer has to separately dismiss a review prompt. `VerdictSheet` takes a `context: VerdictSheetContext` (`FINISHED` / `EDIT`) that swaps copy only — the eyebrow, the headline naming the book, the description, the dismiss label, and the save label — never the anatomy, so the sheet reads as one component with two voices rather than two components. Both the rating and the review are a **local draft** inside the sheet: nothing is written until Save, so a reader can leave with just a rating, just a review, both, or neither. `VerdictBlock` is read-only end to end — it never lets the reader change the rating or the review inline; tapping it (or its "Edit" link, once a verdict exists) is the only way back into the sheet, always in `EDIT` context.
- **Verdict prompt (open-on-finish).** `VerdictSheet` in `FINISHED` context is **cross-feature** — the one modal surface openable from both `feature/book_detail` (already there — reopened from the book page) and `feature/reading` (raised once, automatically, right after the reader marks a book Read). It rises only on a **genuine** `ShelfMutationOutcome.Applied` transition — re-tapping an already-Read book (a no-op mutation) must never reopen it — so both features inspect the mark-as-read use case's outcome before flipping the state that opens the sheet, rather than opening unconditionally on click. On Reading, the prompt opens *after* the existing mark-as-read celebration (haptic + confetti +, when motion is enabled, the row's slide-to-shelf animation) settles, never fighting it for the frame; there is no Delete action in this context (a book that just finished has no existing review yet). It is deliberately **not** wired to the bulk mark-as-read path (Library's multi-select) — prompting once per book in a batch action would be a wall of sheets, not a moment. Because the sheet is a shared `core:designsystem` component fed by each feature's own TOAD state (the same shared-stateless-component shape as `ChooseListsBottomSheet`), and the combined rating+review save is centralised in `core:book`'s `SaveBookVerdictUseCase`, neither feature re-implements the two-call save or the sheet's anatomy — only the trigger (mark-as-read outcome) and the hosting state differ.
- **Reserved-row card.** Carousel and grid cards reserve vertical space for every optional row they may show, so the row of cards never reflows as data streams in.
- **Tonal grouping.** When two regions need to feel distinct on the same page, step the container shade rather than drawing a divider or adding a card outline.
- **Inline filter chip strip.** A horizontally scrolling `Row` of pill-shaped chips placed directly above a grid or list to scope its contents. Selected chip swaps to `secondaryContainer` / `onSecondaryContainer`; unselected sits on `surfaceContainer` / `onSurfaceVariant`. Always include a leading "All …" chip that clears the filter. Reuse the same chip anatomy as the shelf tab pills (§3.4); do not apply the carousel page-edge serif hint (§3.4) — the chip strip is a filter affordance, not a peek-affordance. On an **expanded** (desktop / large) window the strip **wraps** (`FlowRow`) rather than scrolling horizontally, since a pointer can't fling a chip row sideways on a fixed-width pane; compact/medium keep the horizontal scroll. Library's former example of this pattern — a standalone "year finished" chip row above the Read shelf's grid — was retired in the masthead redesign below; that facet now lives as a single-select section inside the Filter sheet (`LibraryFilterOptions.readYears`), alongside the shelf's other facets, rather than as its own chrome row.
- **Active-filter chip row.** Distinct from the inline filter chip strip above: this row renders only the *currently applied* filters as removable input chips, with the picker living in a separate modal sheet. The label sits on the start, and a trailing `ic_close` glyph (14dp) marks it removable on tap. Chips enter and leave via `expandHorizontally` + `fadeIn` / `shrinkHorizontally` + `fadeOut` so the mutation reads as part of the surface's editorial register, not as a pop-in. When more than one chip is active, append a tonal "Clear all" chip on `surfaceContainer` / `onSurfaceVariant` as the row's final element. On an **expanded** (desktop / large) window the row **wraps** (`FlowRow`) instead of scrolling horizontally — a fixed-width pane can't fling a chip row sideways with a pointer — while compact/medium keep the horizontal scroll; this mirrors the filter sheet's panel-form wrap (§3.5). Reach for this row whenever the picker UI is decoupled from the active set — multi-facet library filters, future faceted searches, anywhere the question "what's narrowing my view right now?" needs to be one-tap reversible. The picker itself follows §3.5's modal sheet anatomy with one `FacetSection` per facet (Title label + horizontal pill row), populated only from facet values actually present in the surface's source content so chips never narrow to empty. **Chip fill is a per-consumer choice, not fixed by the pattern:** Library's row uses `primaryContainer` / `onPrimaryContainer` (matching the redesign's token map); a future consumer may keep `secondaryContainer` / `onSecondaryContainer` if that reads better against its own chrome — pick whichever container/on-container pair contrasts against the surface the chips sit on.
- **Book-detail tag block.** The read-only display of a book's tags, in the **The Book lens's `Tags` section**, grouped into the three Hardcover tag categories — in order **Genre**, **Mood**, **Content warning** — each showing the top 5 tags by popularity (`taggable_counts.count`). It sits after the About section. A bar-less in-flow `TAGS` label (§2.3's no-bar register) heads the block; each category gets a `labelLarge` semibold `onSurfaceVariant` sub-label over a **wrapping `FlowRow`** (8dp horizontal + vertical spacing) of read-only `PillChip`s — *not* the horizontally-scrolling `Row` the library facet sections use. The wrap is deliberate: the set is capped at 5 per category and lives in a full-width scrolling column, so all chips should be visible at once rather than hidden behind a scroll affordance. (This is the app's first `FlowRow` usage; reach for the same wrapping block for any capped, full-width set of label chips.) **Content-warning chips are concealed by default:** each renders at its true size but under a solid `spoilerCover` block (clipped to the pill shape), revealed in place — one chip per tap — exactly like the inline review spoiler reveal (§5 / `ReviewDocumentText`), since a content warning can itself be a spoiler. Each chip's reveal is held in `rememberSaveable` keyed by tag id, so an unveiled chip stays unveiled when the section scrolls out of the `LazyColumn` and back (a plain `remember` would re-veil on every recycle). Genre and Mood chips are shown plainly. The section folds in the **edition colophon line** (below) as its trailing element rather than as a separate strip; the whole section — chips and colophon both — is skipped only when there are neither tags nor a known colophon fact, so a book with only a colophon still gets a `Tags` section.
- **Your tags section.** The book-detail display of the user's *own* tags, in the **Yours lens** — distinct from the read-only, popularity-ranked editorial **Book-detail tag block** above. It sits **directly below the in-progress/status block and above the review**, and renders **only when the book is on a shelf** (`book.userBook != null`) — tagging is offered for shelved books only. A bar-less in-flow `YOUR TAGS` label (§2.3's no-bar register) heads it; the tags render as a **single flat wrapping `FlowRow`** of read-only `PillChip`s (8dp spacing) — *not* grouped per category like the editorial block, since a reader's set is small and personal. The user's own tags show **plainly** (spoiler-flagged ones included — this is the reader's private view; the spoiler flag only hides them from *other* readers on the share card). The row ends with a **dashed-`outline`, primary-labelled opener chip** (`DashedTagOpenerChip` — a 1dp dashed round-rect stroke, transparent fill, primary `labelMedium` text) reading **`Edit tags`**, that opens the **Tag editor sheet** (§4); when the user has no tags yet the section shows a single dashed **`+ Add tags`** opener instead — distinct from the solid `PillChip` used for the read-only tags themselves, so the "add/manage" affordance never reads as just another tag. Edits are **optimistic**: the new full set lands in state at once, then the whole set is re-sent through the `upsert_tags` mutation (the backend replaces the complete set, so a removal is "save without it" and a spoiler toggle is "save with the flag flipped"), and the canonical server response overwrites state on success or rolls back on failure (the global error toast already fires). Reach for this pattern for any user-owned tagging surface; keep it separate from the editorial tag block.
- **Masthead-as-shelf-switcher (Library, mobile).** Four rows of chrome (eyebrow, pill tab row, search toggle, sort/filter/layout strip) collapse to two: the page title *is* the shelf switcher. Anatomy, top to bottom, inside the page's collapsible header block: eyebrow (`PullToRefreshEyebrow`, unchanged) → a **title row** pairing the shelf name (`pageTitle`, autosized down via `TextAutoSize.StepBased` for a long name, clamped to 3 lines) with a trailing primary chevron-down (24dp, top-aligned) → the stat subtitle (`body`, `onSurfaceVariant`, unchanged `subtitleFor` copy). The whole title row is one tap target (`pressScaleClickable`) that opens the **Shelves sheet** below — this *replaces* the pill tab row entirely; there is no swipe-between-shelves gesture and no `HorizontalPager`, so a shelf renders only its own content. Below the (still scroll-collapsible) title block sits the **persistent** `EditorialSearchField` (§4, no toggle) and then the **control line** (below). **Desktop keeps its permanent shelf sidebar as the switcher instead** (§2.7/§5 expanded-width source-list pattern) — no title chevron, no Shelves sheet there; the sidebar already gives a wide window a switcher, and duplicating a sheet-based one would be the phone-derived pattern the desktop redesign retires elsewhere. Persistent search and the control line are shared by both platforms.
- **Masthead control line.** The redesign's replacement for the retired view-control strip: one row, both platforms, sitting directly beneath the persistent search field. **Left:** the current sort mode's label (`eyebrowSmall`, `onSurfaceVariant`, uppercase) plus a 16dp primary asc/desc chevron — hidden under a positional sort (Manual/Order, where the stored order *is* the sort, so a direction arrow is meaningless) — tapping either opens the **Arrange sheet** (below). Immediately beside it, an inline **"Reorder"/"Done" hint chip** (drag-handle glyph + label, `surfaceContainer`/`onSurfaceVariant` idle, `secondaryContainer`/`onSecondaryContainer` while engaged) appears only when the current tab is on a reorderable positional sort with ≥2 visible items — this is the control line's version of the old strip's trailing rearrange icon-button; see "Rearrange mode" below. **Right:** a `surfaceContainer` **Filter pill** (17dp filter glyph + "Filter" label, `inversePrimary` dot when a filter is active) opening the Filter sheet, and a 38dp `surfaceContainer` **Select circle** (checkbox glyph) that enters selection mode with an **empty** selection — unlike a cover long-press, which seeds the pressed cover, the toolbar has no single book to anchor to, so it opens empty and the user picks their own start (`OnEnterSelectionModeAction`'s `bookId` is nullable for exactly this: `null` from the Select circle, the pressed cover's id from a long-press). The Select circle is omitted on custom-list tabs (editions don't carry shelf-selection semantics) and on an empty shelf. Unlike the old strip, there is **no layout affordance on the control line at all** — layout now lives inside the Arrange sheet alongside sort. 24dp horizontal page gutter; hides during bulk-select mode (selection has its own header) with the same `expandVertically`/`shrinkVertically` register the chip rows use.
- **Shelves sheet (Library, mobile).** Opened by tapping the masthead title (above); the sheet form of the shelf switcher. Canonical editorial header (accent bar + `YOUR SHELVES` eyebrow + headline), with a quiet trailing "Manage" text affordance in the header row that routes to the shelf-visibility settings screen — the sheet's replacement for the old tab-row long-press entry point, which must survive somewhere now that there is no tab row to long-press. Below the header, one hairline-split row per `LibraryUiState.visibleTabs`: a leading glyph (a shelf icon, `onSurfaceVariant`, for the built-in All/status shelves; a list icon, `primary`, for custom lists — built-in shelves and custom lists render as *equals*, distinguished only by this glyph), the shelf name (`titleLarge` forced italic, clamp 2 lines, `primary` when it's the active shelf), a trailing tabular item count (`tabStatsFor`), and a check glyph on the active row. Tapping a row dispatches the tab-select action and dismisses. Desktop never shows this sheet (its sidebar is always visible).
- **Arrange sheet.** Replaces the old sort dropdown *and* layout dropdown with one sheet, shared by mobile and desktop (opened from the masthead control line above, or the desktop equivalent). Canonical editorial header (`ARRANGE THE SHELF`), then two sections: **LAYOUT** — three fully-rounded chips (Grid·2 / Grid·3 / List; active `primary`/`onPrimary`, idle `surfaceContainer`/`onSurface`) that are a pure UI bucketing over the existing persisted grid-layout enum (no new persisted field), followed by a "Show titles & authors" `Switch` row (Fraunces italic label) that's hidden on the List chip and otherwise flips the enum's titled/cover-only sibling — and **SORT BY · {shelf name}** — the shelf's gated, ordered sort-mode list as chips (same anatomy as the layout chips); tapping the already-active chip flips its direction (a trailing chevron on the active chip signals which way, suppressed for Manual/Order). **Draft/commit:** layout, titled-ness, and sort mode/direction are a local draft — seeded from the committed grid layout / sort mode / direction the moment the sheet opens — that every chip tap and the titles toggle mutate in place; nothing reaches state until the footer button fires `OnApplyArrangeAction`, which commits all three atomically, and dismissing any other way (scrim, back) discards the draft untouched. The one exception is **"✶ Make this list ordered"**, which appears beneath an unranked custom list's chips and stays live/immediate on tap (`OnSetListRankedAction`, the same one the old sort dropdown's footer item dispatched) since it flips a structural list flag rather than a sort/layout preference — the draft's sort mode is nudged to `ORDER` right after so the chip row doesn't look stale for the rest of the visit. A full-width filled `RhaydusButton` footer ("Show N titles", previewing the *already-committed* count — sort/layout never change how many titles show) commits-and-dismisses, sized `ButtonSize.M` on **both** sheet and panel forms (not the L-on-sheet/M-on-panel split the Update-progress sheet uses — L reads too tall for this footer on either form). Desktop's grid still renders at an adaptive column count regardless of the Grid·2-vs-3 chip choice (§3.6 large-window list/grid rule) — the chip only round-trips the enum value, which the desktop grid maps by titled-vs-cover-only and grid-vs-list, not by literal column count.
- **Filter sheet.** The restyled facet picker (canonical header, `FILTER THIS SHELF`): a merged **Ownership · Format** section, **Release year**, **Year finished** (Read tab only, from `LibraryFilterOptions.readYears`), a **Tags** section, then **Rating** — each a `FacetSection` of `PillChip`s, wrapping (`ExpandableFlowRow`) on the panel form and horizontally scrolling on the sheet form (§3.5). The Tags section additionally carries a trailing count reading **"N available"** — the number of distinct tags the shelf's books carry, *not* how many the user has selected (which would read wrong at "1 in use" with a dozen more to browse) — and a search-your-tags pill that narrows the chip cloud by substring, client-side, as transient local state (not part of the TOAD contract). That pill is a hand-composed leading-icon + `BasicTextField` pill rather than the foundation `EditorialSearchField`: that component hardcodes its own 24dp horizontal padding and a ~56dp height meant to sit directly on a page, which — nested inside this sheet's already-24dp-padded facet column — would both double-inset the pill past the other facets' left edge and blow past the sheet's tighter search-pill height. **Draft/commit:** every facet chip and the in-sheet "Clear all" mutate a local `LibraryFilters` draft, seeded from the tab's committed filters the moment the sheet opens; the footer's "Show N titles" count previews *that draft* live (plus the current search) via `libraryPreviewCount`, so it always matches what committing would actually show. Nothing reaches state until "Show N titles" fires `OnApplyFiltersAction` with the whole draft and dismisses; dismissing any other way (scrim, back) discards the draft untouched. This is deliberately separate from the main-screen active-filter chip row, which stays live/immediate (`OnToggleFilterValueAction` / `OnClearFiltersAction`) since it has no commit step of its own. The footer: a full-width `ButtonSize.M` "Show N titles" button, beside an **always-rendered** "Clear all" text affordance (clearing the draft, not dispatching) that dims to ~0.4 alpha and goes fully inert — no click handler, no desktop hand cursor — when the draft has no active filter, rather than appearing/disappearing, so applying the first filter never shoves the primary button sideways.
- **Library layout model.** The redesign's three Arrange-sheet layout chips (Grid·2 / Grid·3 / List) and "Show titles & authors" toggle are pure UI mappings over Library's existing six-value grid-layout enum — no persisted-model change. "Show titles" reflects whether the current layout is a titled grid or its cover-only sibling; toggling it flips between the two, preserving the Grid·2-vs-3 choice. Picking a layout chip preserves whichever titled-ness was already set. The List chip always resolves to the large (de-carded) row layout; the enum's older compact-list value is **no longer offered** by the picker — it is kept only so a value some client previously persisted still renders (as the large-row layout, not a blank fallback), not as an option a user can newly choose.
- **Library cover grid & de-carded list row.** The grid/cover-only cells (§3.4-style anatomy, unchanged cover-then-caption order) add two rows around the cover, both height-reserved so a shelf mixing in-progress and untouched books never jumps: a countdown deadline badge overlaid top-start on the cover itself (only while the book has a tracked deadline — see the Deadline badge entry in §4), and a thin `LinearWavyProgressIndicator` line immediately beneath the cover (`primary` indicator, `outlineVariant` track, 8dp reserved height) while the book is in progress — reading Library's specific instance of the app-wide "progress is always wavy" rule (§6). The redesign's List (large) row is **de-carded**: the pre-redesign `surfaceContainer` rounded card is dropped in favour of a hairline `outlineVariant` top divider and `18dp` vertical row padding, matching the flattened anatomy the rest of the shelf now reads with (masthead, control line, chip row — no boxed regions). Byline reads `BY {AUTHOR}` in `eyebrowSmall` (uppercase, `onSurfaceVariant`) rather than the plain sentence-case "By {author}" other surfaces use. In progress, the same wavy line (120dp wide) sits beneath the meta line with a tabular `NN%` beside it; the row shows **no** deadline badge (the wave already carries reading state) and no longer carries the pre-redesign `DeadlineSummaryLine` — a book's deadline date remains visible on book detail. **This byline treatment is no longer Library-exclusive:** the Hidden Suggestions screen's hairline book row (§5 "Settings menu row"-style anatomy) reuses the same `BY {AUTHOR}` `eyebrowSmall` byline, since that row sits in the same flat, hairline-separated register this one does and a full sentence read as too heavy beside the compact cover. Reach for it on any other hairline-row book listing in this same flattened register; a boxed-card or carousel row should keep the plain sentence-case byline.
- **Pace-nudge ribbon.** A dismissable banner **fused to the top of the card it speaks to** — not an in-flow row above it (the pre-redesign anatomy; the Reading 1a redesign moved it onto the card itself so the message reads as the card's own voice rather than page chrome). `primaryContainer` / `onPrimaryContainer` fill, 13/18dp padding, a leading info glyph, the italic message in `bodySmall`, and a trailing × dismiss icon-button (no copy beyond the message itself). Used on the Reading screen's featured-hero card to surface a per-day pacing target derived from the featured book's active deadline. Persistence is per-book-per-day; once dismissed, the ribbon stays gone until the local date rolls over. Because it is the card's own top band, the outer card's rounded clip gives it matching top corners for free — it never declares its own shape. Reach for it when a single sentence of guidance derives from existing state, is scoped to one card, and should not earn the weight of a sheet.
- **Actionable inline banner.** The nudge's heavier sibling: when an in-flow prompt needs a *primary action* (not just acknowledgement), promote it to a `surfaceContainerHigh` rounded card (24dp page gutter, 16dp radius) holding an eyebrow (`eyebrowSmall`, `primary`), one italic `bodySmall` sentence, and a single tonal `RhaydusButton` (`ButtonSize.S`) — with a trailing `ic_close` `IconButton` to dismiss. Used on book detail when arriving from a barcode scan and the book is already on a shelf with a *different* edition ("Already on your shelves — update it to the one you scanned?"); the button routes to the edition-update action, dismiss hides the offer while the scanned edition stays displayed. It sits **directly beneath the shelf-action section** it speaks to, not above it. While the action is in flight, the tonal button is **replaced** by a 20dp `CircularWavyProgressIndicator` + an "Updating edition…" `bodySmall`/`onSurfaceVariant` line — this both signals progress and removes the tap target so the action can't be fired twice (the action also self-guards on its in-progress flag). One tonal action per banner (never two competing buttons); the banner is rendered as its own list item and omitted entirely (not space-reserved) when its condition is false. Reach for it when a transient, condition-gated prompt carries a concrete next step; reach for the lighter pacing nudge when no action is offered.
- **Shelve control.** The book-detail shelf-status control: a bar-less inline-bar-headed (§2.3) `SHELVE THIS BOOK` label over a `surfaceContainerLow`, 15dp-radius card with 5dp inner padding and a 2dp gap between three **vertical rows** — Want to read / Reading / Read — replacing an earlier horizontal chip strip. Each row is an 11dp-radius, 11×13dp-padded `Row` (18dp leading icon, `labelLarge` name, optional trailing live status) that fills `primary`/`onPrimary` when active and otherwise sits transparent on the card. The active **Reading** row shows a live trailing status (`{pct}% · p. {page}`, time-based for audiobooks); the active **Read** row shows the read date when known. All existing behavior carries over unchanged: optimistic writes, `Modifier.shakeOnError` + "Couldn't save — tap to retry" on the card label, the `MarkAsReadBurst` + commit haptic celebration on Read, and Reading disabled while the shelf status is `None`. While the book is loading, the card's `SkeletonCrossfade` shows three shimmer bars built from the *same* outer card (`surfaceContainerLow`, 5dp padding, 2dp gaps) and the *same* per-row height/radius as the loaded rows — never a single oversized blob — so the shimmer resolves into the real rows at an identical height instead of visibly shrinking.
- **Lens toggle.** A sticky, full-width segmented pill (`surfaceContainer` track, `999` radius, 4dp padding) that switches book detail's lower content between two lenses, leading with **The Book** (the book's own facts: about, tags, find-it, voices) then **Yours** (the reader's own copy: in-progress/status, rating, tags, review) — the always-available segment leads since Yours can be disabled — riding a `stickyHeader` immediately under the top bar so it stays pinned while the lens content scrolls beneath it. Both segments are always visible; **Yours is disabled** (dimmed, non-interactive) until the book has a user copy (`book.userBook != null`), and **The Book** is the default selection. The active segment fills `primary`/`onPrimary` and crossfades over ~220ms (gated by `playDecorativeMotion()`); switching fires a `select` haptic. The content beneath crossfades with the same gate — sections belonging to the inactive lens are simply not composed, not hidden. Reach for this pattern wherever a detail surface needs to separate a reader's private data from the entity's public facts without splitting into two screens; do not build a second bespoke segmented switcher.
- **Book-detail in-progress stat.** The Reading-shelf specialization of the app's **Hero stat** pattern (above): a `SectionLabel("In progress")` opener with a trailing `Update` pill (`surfaceContainerHigh` fill, `primary` content, 15dp pencil icon, opens the Update progress sheet) beside it, then the percentage as a `statHero` numeral, an M3-expressive **`LinearWavyProgressIndicator`** immediately beneath it (never the flat `LinearProgressIndicator` — the app-wide "wavy always" rule, §6, applies here too), a `p. {x} of {y}` / `{n} pages to go` counts row (italic `bodySmall`, `onSurfaceVariant`, space-between), an optional **reading-pace forecast** line ("Finish in {x} reading days", derived from the reader's actual per-reading-day pace and hidden entirely when a forecast can't be computed — fewer than one logged reading day, no progress, or already finished), and the existing `DeadlineRow` beneath when a deadline is set. Bar and number read as one motion, as the Hero stat pattern requires. The pace-forecast line and `DeadlineRow` each arrive on their own timer (a separate network round trip and a separate collector, respectively) well after the progress block above has settled, and either may never arrive at all for a given book — so **neither reserves height**; both reveal in place with `expandVertically() + fadeIn()` / `shrinkVertically() + fadeOut()` (gated by `playDecorativeMotion()`, an instant swap under reduced motion) rather than popping in and shoving whatever follows down in a single frame. This is the general rule for any optional trailing line that frequently never arrives: animate the reveal, don't reserve it; a line that (almost) always arrives, or whose absence would otherwise leave a jarring blank run, reserves its height instead — see the Reading screen's pace-nudge ribbon and the Shelve control's per-row trailing status for that side of the same choice. **The underlying reading-pace forecast (`ReadingPaceForecast.compute`, its use case, repository, and remote data source) is a shared `core:personal` capability**, not a book_detail internal — the Reading screen's featured-hero card (below) consumes the same forecast for its own "Your pace" line, computed by a Reading-local collector over the featured book only; each screen still owns its own copy phrasing and reveal choreography for the number.
- **Standfirst.** A book's `headline` — a one- or two-line editorial introduction owned by the book itself (not the edition) — rendered as a magazine *standfirst* (the bold lead between a title and its body). It opens the book-detail **about section**: directly under the `About` `SectionLabel`, set in the editorial `bodyLarge` role (italic Fraunces, `onSurface`) so it reads as a subtitle/lead rather than a competing headline, with a gap (≈16dp) before the drop-cap description body begins. It sits in the editorial italic voice deliberately — a roman title/`titleMedium` register would read as structural chrome, and a `headline`-scale role oversizes a one-sentence intro into three wrapped lines. The title lives in the cover hero above, so the standfirst is the page's first on-`surface` editorial voice and leads the eye straight into the description — title → standfirst → body, the classic spread rhythm. It is plain text, never run through `htmlToAnnotatedString` (that path is for the server-HTML description below it). Suppressed when the book has no headline; when a headline exists but the description is empty, the standfirst stands alone and the "No description…" fallback is omitted. Reach for it wherever a record carries a short curated lead distinct from its long body; do not promote it to its own section header or reuse the section-headline `display` role (that would compete with the section eyebrow).
- **Edition colophon line.** A single-line `bodySmall` / `onSurfaceVariant` string that surfaces print-edition specifics — publisher, format + release year, and ISBN-13 today, scoped to the user's currently-selected edition — folded in as the trailing element of the **Book-detail tag block**'s `Tags` section (above), not a standalone strip. Parts are joined with a ` · ` separator; the ISBN-13 carries its own inline `ISBN-13` label as a prefix on the value (no eyebrow, no separate metadata table). Any part whose fact is unknown is dropped rather than shown blank; the whole line is omitted when none of the three parts is known. Reach for this pattern when a screen needs to name a physical-edition fact in passing without earning a section header or its own card; do not promote it to a labelled metadata table.
- **External-links strip (labeled pills).** A bar-less in-flow `FIND IT` label (§2.3's no-bar register) followed by a wrapping `FlowRow` of **labeled outline pills** — not icon-only buttons — that hand off to off-app destinations. Used on book detail (The Book lens) under the tags/colophon stack to route the reader to a print/library lookup for the currently-selected edition — Bookshop, Amazon, OpenLibrary, all keyed off the edition's ISBN as a search query (prefer `edition.isbn13`, fall back to `edition.isbn10`). Each pill is a fully-rounded (`999`) transparent `Surface` with a hairline `outlineVariant` border, a 16dp primary-tinted leading icon (`ic_storefront`, `ic_shopping_bag`, `ic_library_books`), and a `labelMedium` `onSurface` destination name (`Bookshop` / `Amazon` / `OpenLibrary`) — 16×10dp padding, 8dp icon-label gap. Naming the destination in text (rather than relying on the glyph alone) is the deliberate change from the old icon-only strip: the brand identity isn't carried by the glyph, so the earlier row needed a verbose `contentDescription` to be legible at all — the label now carries that legibility visually, and the `contentDescription` stays as the accessibility-only backstop. The handoff itself goes through a `UiEvent` (the section dispatches an `OnExternalLinkClickAction` carrying the URL; the screen's top-level `Content()` collects the resulting `OpenExternalLinkEvent` and invokes `LocalUriHandler.openUri` there) — never call `LocalUriHandler` from inside the section, or any other leaf composable. The entire section — label included — is suppressed when the current edition has no ISBN, since every pill would be dead. Reach for this pattern wherever a detail surface needs to launch the reader into a small, fixed set of off-app lookups for the entity in view; do not stretch it into a generic "share/open in…" sheet — for that, reach for the share-sheet pattern.
- **Book-detail lens-section reveal.** The Book lens's Tags and Find it sections are both condition-gated — neither ISBN nor community tags/colophon are known before the book resolves, and either may genuinely never exist for a given book — so, like the actionable inline banner, neither reserves a skeleton. What they don't get away with is popping straight into an otherwise-settled column: each wraps its content in `AnimatedVisibility(enter = expandVertically() + fadeIn(), exit = ExitTransition.None)` (gated by `playDecorativeMotion()`), composed unconditionally so the false→true flip actually plays the enter transition rather than materializing pre-visible. Voices is the opposite case — a book (almost) always has *some* community reception once it resolves, and its own fetch (`loadingReviews`) trails the rest of the book by a separate round trip — so it reserves instead: a `SkeletonCrossfade` holds the "Voices" header static and shimmers two `surfaceContainerLow`, 20dp-radius, 150dp placeholder cards for the whole loading span, collapsing to nothing only once loading finishes with zero reviews. Unlike the Shelve control's dimensionally-exact skeleton (§5 "Shelve control"), this is a deliberate **approximation** — real review cards run taller (up to 8 body lines plus a footer row) and the eventual count is rarely exactly two — so it follows `AboutSection`'s looser idiom (a placeholder that stands in for "content is coming," not a pixel-matched preview) rather than promising an exact-height match.
- **Lens-toggle enable fade.** The toggle's disabled "Yours" segment (above) flips enabled the instant a shelved book lands — a data-driven change arriving alongside everything else on the page settling at once. Its dim state is not a bare `Modifier.alpha` snap: the alpha rides `animateFloatAsState` on the same tween/snap spec as the segment's own selection crossfade, so the flip reads as a settle, not a flash.
- **Adaptive empty state.** When a root surface is empty, surface the next-best content rather than a static "nothing here" panel. The empty Reading screen demonstrates the rule: if Want-to-Read has entries, render the top three as a "Pick up next" tile row (96dp covers + title only); else, render a single "Trending now" tile that links into book detail. Pair with the existing empty-state quote glyph + headline (§5 editorial quote, empty-state variant); do not stack three carousels — the empty state is a moment of orientation, not a discovery surface.
- **Bulk-select mode.** Long-press a cover on a built-in shelf tab (All / Want-to-Read / Currently Reading / Read) to enter selection mode — **on desktop, where long-press is invisible to a pointer, entry is via right-click → "Select" or Ctrl/Cmd-click, with Shift-click range select (§2.5 desktop selection); the desktop right-click menu also exposes the per-book operations directly.** The masthead control line's Select circle (above) is a third entry point — it opens with an empty selection rather than seeding a book, since it has no pressed cover to anchor to. Fire `Haptics.threshold()` at activation, `Haptics.select()` on every subsequent toggle, `Haptics.commit()` when the user applies a bulk action. The page header swaps to a **selection header**: a 38dp `surfaceContainerHigh` × circle to exit, an italic `headlineSmall` "N selected" count, then a row of labelled 44dp `surfaceContainerHigh` action pills — **Move** (icon + label, opens a dropdown of move-shelf targets) and **Add to list** (icon + label, opens the shared `ChooseListsBottomSheet`) sharing the row's width, plus a 44dp `surfaceContainerHigh` trash circle (`primary` glyph, opens a confirmation `AlertDialog`). The masthead title/chevron, persistent search, control line, and filter chip row all hide while in selection mode. Back press, the exit circle, switching tabs, and deselecting the last book all exit. Per-cover anatomy: unselected covers dim to ~0.55 alpha; a selected cover gets an inset 3dp `primary` ring + a 16% `primary` wash over the (undimmed) cover, plus a 26dp circular badge at the top-end (filled `primary` with `ic_check` for selected, translucent `surface` ring for unselected) — the ring+wash is new in the redesign, layered under the same badge the pre-redesign selection already used. For `LIST_COMPACT` rows (kept only for back-compat — see "Library layout model" below, the Arrange sheet no longer offers it) the badge stays a leading position before the title with no ring/wash. The add-to-list sheet uses the same surface as book detail, generalised to accept a `Set<bookId>` and to render tristate membership per list (none / partial / all — tapping commits toward the larger end, matching the standard tristate-checkbox UX); after a successful toggle the surface stays in selection mode so consecutive adds across multiple lists feel like one operation. Custom-list tabs (which render editions rather than books) do not opt into selection — long-press is a no-op there, and the control line's Select circle is omitted on those tabs. Reach for this pattern whenever a root list surface needs N-target operations; do not invent a per-screen variant.
- **Rearrange mode.** A transient editing mode that gates drag-to-reorder behind an explicit opt-in, so a positional sort (MANUAL on a built-in shelf, ORDER on a ranked custom list) renders *display-only* by default — normal scroll/tap/long-press, no drag handles — and the order can't be nudged while browsing. Entered and exited from the **inline "Reorder"/"Done" hint chip** in the masthead control line (see "Masthead control line" above): it appears only when the current tab is on a positional sort with ≥2 visible items, reads as plain chrome when idle and fills to `secondaryContainer`/`onSecondaryContainer` while rearranging — the one chip is both the entry and the `Done` exit. Entering reveals the per-card drag handles (see the two "Drag-to-reorder … grid variant" patterns for handle anatomy and persistence); the mode is **drag-only** — tap-to-open and long-press-to-select are suppressed so the gesture vocabulary stays unambiguous while handles are live. Crucially, entering/leaving the mode must **not** swap the grid for a different composable: the display and reorder states are rendered by the **same** grid node (items only gain a handle and drop their tap targets), because disposing one grid and composing another remounts every cover's image and flashes the screen. Exits on the button (now `Done`), system back, switching tabs, or changing the sort. It is **mutually exclusive with bulk-select** (entering either clears the other), and it is the editing-mode sibling of "Bulk-select mode" above — reach for it whenever a surface offers an in-place reorder that would otherwise let casual scrolling perturb the order; do not leave always-live drag handles on a freely-scrolling grid.
- **Library tabs reorderable row.** The Library-tabs list under Settings → Library tabs: one flat, borderless `Column` of rows (radius 12dp, no card, no hairline dividers between rows) — retiring the earlier tonal `surfaceContainer` / `RoundedCornerShape(20dp)` grouped-list treatment (`SettingsGroup` / `SettingsRowDivider`, now removed), since a list mixing a fixed leading row, drag-to-reorder, and a per-row show/hide affordance reads flatter and lighter this way, matching the rest of Settings. The screen opens once with the canonical `EditorialSectionHeader`, then a bar-less group eyebrow + italic subhead (the "a list" legend's dot inlined mid-sentence via `InlineTextContent`, not a separate leading glyph) — no second accent bar, since that would be group-level ceremony the header above already spent. **Row anatomy:** a leading 26dp grip/pin column — a `primary`-while-dragging `ic_drag_handle` glyph (`onSurfaceVariant` idle) for a reorderable entry, or a demoted (`onSurfaceVariant` @ 0.5 alpha) pin glyph for the one fixed front row that can neither move nor be moved past (the built-in "All" shelf) — then the name (Inter, roman, a leading 6dp `primary` dot for a custom list, none for a built-in shelf) over an italic Fraunces tabular count line ("N titles", swapped for "Hidden from tabs" while the row is hidden), and a trailing 38dp circular eye toggle (filled `primary` eye when shown, outline struck-eye when hidden) — the row's only show/hide affordance, replacing both the earlier per-row `Switch` and any overflow menu, and present only on a row that can be hidden at all (the fixed "All" row and an always-visible status such as Currently Reading carry none). A hidden row drops to 52% opacity. Because no single tap opens a row — only the grip drags and the eye toggles — the row's "press wash" (`animateColorAsState` to `surfaceContainer`, gated by `playDecorativeMotion()`) reflects the row's own drag state rather than a tap, washing only while its grip is picked up. **Drag mechanics:** pressing the handle picks the row up immediately (no long-press; the handle *is* the affordance) — fire `Haptics.lift()` (§2.5), tint the handle `primary`, and translate the row vertically with the drag via `graphicsLayer { translationY }`; the other rows stay put during the drag (no intermediate-slot snapping) and only shift once the drop target is computed by walking neighbouring row heights past their midpoint, firing `Haptics.drop()` on release. Gesture wiring is `Modifier.draggable(orientation = Vertical, startDragImmediately = true)`, applied only to a reorderable row's grip column, so it composes with a vertically-scrolling parent without fighting it; the fixed front row carries no draggable modifier at all and the drop-target computation is clamped so nothing can land above it. A full-width **New list** foot action closes the list: a 34dp `primaryContainer` circular plus tile beside a `primary` label, opening the list-creation flow. Long-press *outside* the row (e.g. on a tab pill in the Library strip) remains the canonical shortcut into this screen — fire `Haptics.threshold()` and route here rather than spawning an inline reorder mode. Reach for this anatomy for any other settings-style list that mixes a fixed leading row, drag-to-reorder, and a per-row show/hide toggle.
- **Drag-to-reorder shelf (grid variant — built-in shelves, MANUAL).** Used on the built-in Library shelves (Want-to-Read, Currently Reading, Read) when the tab's sort mode is `MANUAL`. MANUAL is offered in the Arrange sheet's sort chips only for those three shelves — the All tab and Did Not Finish suppress it; custom-list tabs use the separate `ORDER` mode below — and its chip carries no direction chevron because the stored position itself is the order. **Selecting MANUAL is display-only:** the saved order renders with normal scroll, tap-to-open, and long-press-to-select intact and *no drag handles*, so the order can't be nudged while browsing. Dragging is gated behind an explicit **Rearrange mode** (see "Rearrange mode" below), entered from the masthead control line's "Reorder" hint chip. Only while rearranging does each card gain a grab affordance overlaid at its top-end: a circular `surfaceContainerHigh`-tinted pill (~28dp, 4dp padding, alpha 0.9, `onSurfaceVariant` content) wrapping an `ic_drag_handle` glyph, wired via `Modifier.draggableHandle(...)` from the `sh.calvin.reorderable` library. Pressing the handle fires `Haptics.lift()`, the reorder library snaps neighbouring cards into intermediate slots during the drag, and release fires `Haptics.drop()`. Rearrange mode is drag-only — tapping a cover does not navigate and long-press does not enter bulk-select, so the gesture vocabulary stays unambiguous while the handle is live (the long-press → bulk-select shortcut lives on the display-only path instead). Rearrange and bulk-select are mutually exclusive: entering one clears the other. Persistence is local-only and **prefix-scoped**: on drop, only positions `0..maxTouchedIndex` are written, where `maxTouchedIndex` is the deepest visual index any move spanned. Books beyond that depth retain whatever state they had (positioned or unsorted), so a shallow drag at the top of the shelf doesn't pin books the user never touched, and newcomers fetched from the API surface at the top of the unsorted region — immediately below the pinned prefix — rather than at the very bottom. Reach for this pattern wherever a grid surface needs an in-place reorder that coexists with a separate sort dimension and with bulk-select.
- **Reading-session peek bar.** While a reading session is running, a persistent live-timer bar (`SessionPeekBar`, §4) sits directly above the bottom navigation — between the tab content and the nav, in both the docked and floating bar styles — and slides in when a session starts, out when it ends. It is the always-glanceable in-app counterpart to the lock-screen notification — a **plain, persistent ongoing notification** (`BigPictureStyle` edition cover, running chronometer, pause/resume + stop + an inline "update page" reply), hosted by `ReadingSessionService` as a foreground service and kept persistent — since Android 14 ignores `setOngoing` for FGS notifications, a `deleteIntent` re-posts it if the user swipes it away, so it returns and is removed only when the session ends. It is deliberately **not** a media/`MediaSession` notification: that was tried and abandoned because a real audio app (Spotify) wins the single media slot and overwrites it, and because a media session drags in the speaker-output chip and media-button capture. A plain notification always shows independently, touches no audio routing, and never competes for media controls. All three surfaces (peek bar, lock screen, Focus Mode) read and drive the one app-scoped `ActiveSessionController`: same book, same running timer, same pause/resume + stop controls. Tapping it opens Focus Mode. Mount it once at the app shell; never reserve layout space for it when idle (it animates from zero height), and never let a screen render its own session timer — if a surface needs to show "you're reading this now", it reads the controller.
- **Focus Mode.** A distraction-free full-screen reading surface for the active session, pushed over the bottom-bar shell (so the peek bar and nav recede). It is an **editorial hero on the page `surface`** (§3.3), not a dark scrim — a centred column reading top-to-bottom: a `NOW READING` eyebrow (primary), the book cover (subtle radius + shadow, §2.3), the title in `display` (italic, on-surface) over a `By …` byline in `body` (on-surface-variant), then the running timer as a **hero stat** (§5) — an eyebrow label (`READING TIME`, or `PAUSED` in on-surface-variant) above the `statHero` numeral (a formatted `H:MM:SS` string driven by a one-second `LaunchedEffect`, dimmed to on-surface-variant while paused; `AnimatedStatNumber` is for live integers, not a composite time string, so it does not apply here). Below sits the editable page as an **editable hero stat** (`HeroStatNumberField` + `EditorialSuffix` "of N pages" + a progress bar — the same treatment as the progress sheet's page tab; it commits the new page on focus loss through the same `RecordBookProgressUseCase` the reading screen uses, and falls back to a read-only "% complete" for books without a page count), then a pause/resume (`FILLED`) + stop (`OUTLINED`) `RhaydusButton` row. All copy uses editorial roles on the page surface so it stays legible — never dark-on-dark. It owns no domain state beyond a transient page-input buffer, so it reads `ActiveSessionController` directly rather than carrying a TOAD ScreenModel, and pops itself the instant no session is active. Reached three ways — the peek-bar tap, the featured-card "Focus mode" button, and the lock-screen notification's content tap (deep-linked through a `singleTop` `MainActivity` into a consumable focus-mode flag the root observes). Reach for this register when a session wants the whole screen; do not build a second full-screen timer.
- **Streak strip.** A compact 21-day reading-activity row sitting in the Reading screen header, directly below the greeting subtitle. Days run **most-recent-first** — today at the left edge, older days trailing right — inside a **horizontally scrolling** `Row` (`Modifier.horizontalScroll`, fixed dot size + `spacedBy` gap) so every dot stays comfortably sized rather than cramming 21 into the viewport and clipping the recent end on narrow phones. A **lit** day (the user read anything that day) is a filled `primary` dot; an **unread** day is a faint track dot (`onSurfaceVariant` at ~0.18 alpha); **today** is a slightly larger dot (filled `primary` when read, or a `primary`-ringed track dot when not) anchored at the scroll origin so it reads as the home position. Orientation is carried by a **static, direction-only caption** beneath the dots ("Most recent first", `eyebrowSmall`/`onSurfaceVariant`), right-aligned beside an italic Fraunces `bodySmall`/`primary` **"N days running"** caption — the current streak length, computed as a pure UI-layer helper counting consecutive read days backward from today through the same activity list the dots render (no new domain concept). The caption is omitted (not space-reserved) whenever today itself was missed, since "0 days running" reads as a non-sequitur; the dots-only caption alone still carries the row's orientation in that case. The date label is deliberately *not* a per-end date, since a scrolling row would make any pinned "Today / 10 May" end-marker drift out of sync with the dot it names. The dot is **binary** — it reflects *that day's own* reading activity, fully decoupled from the current streak run (a gap day simply goes dark while earlier read days stay lit). "Read anything" is sourced from the same Hardcover reading-journal dates the Profile streak uses — never from optional reading sessions, which users may not run. The whole row is one `pressScaleClickable` tap target that fires `Haptics.select()` (§2.5) and opens a transient per-day breakdown `AdaptiveModalSheet` that — like every sheet (§3.5) — opens with `EditorialSectionHeader` (a "Last N days" eyebrow + a "you read N of the last 21 days" headline), then one row per day, most-recent first, each with its dot and a relative `Today`/`Yesterday`/`EEE d MMM` label. It is the interim affordance until the full Reading Activity calendar lands. The strip carries a fixed dot-cell height so dots filling in never reflow it; when no day in the window has activity it is **omitted entirely** (not space-reserved) so a new user never sees a row of dead dots — accept the one-time header grow when the first day is logged. Reach for this pattern when a header needs an at-a-glance binary activity history; do not overload the dot with duration/intensity tiers (that is the calendar's job).
- **Reading featured-hero card.** The Reading screen's emotional centre: a `surfaceContainerLow`, 22dp-radius card with its overflow clipped, holding (top→bottom) an optional pace-nudge ribbon fused to the card's own top edge (above) and, beneath it, **one continuous blurred-cover backdrop spanning the entire rest of the card** — cover, meta, the page-count/percentage stat, the wavy bar, the deadline row, both pills, and the caption all sit over the same image, never a backdrop band handed off to a separate flat body surface. **Backdrop** (the pre-redesign treatment, restored after a brief redesign detour tried both a fixed dark-ink field + terracotta radial glow, and later a backdrop band + separate body — neither read right): a full-opacity blurred cover image (blur 64dp), a `Color.Black` top scrim fading out by ~55% down the card (legibility for the series eyebrow near the top edge), and a fade at the foot into the card's own `surfaceContainerLow` — no fixed-ink field, no radial glow, no left→right scrim. Over the backdrop, one content column: the featured cover (108×162, 4dp radius, `DeadlineCoverOverlay`, no cover-edge ornament) beside a text column — a series eyebrow (or the mutation-failed retry line, in `error`, when a save failed), the hero title (`headlineSmall`, italic), the byline, and — only when the collector has a computed forecast for this book — a "Your pace" label-and-rule row over an italic pace line ("35 pages a day — about 5 days to go") — then, below that row, a page-count line (current at full alpha, "of N" demoted to 0.6 alpha) beside the "NN%" stat (`headlineSmall`), an M3-expressive `LinearWavyProgressIndicator` (never flat — §6), an optional calendar-icon + status-word `DeadlineRow` (the app's `OnTrack`/`Behind`/`Expired` labels only — see the Deadline badge entry above; date and pace are not repeated here since the "Your pace" row and Book Detail's `DeadlineRow` already carry them), a filled "Update progress" pill (`primary`/`onPrimary`, unaffected by the backdrop since a filled button carries its own solid fill), the session button (outlined "Start reading session", or tonal "Focus mode" while a session on this book is active — the one place a hero action legitimately swaps style based on live state), and an italic time-of-day caption. **Every one of those lines — meta text, page count, percentage stat, deadline row, caption — is set in the fixed `ReadingHeroBackdropForeground` ink (§2.1)**, not `onSurface`/`onSurfaceVariant`, because the whole column now sits on the image rather than a flat card surface. The outlined session pill is the one catalog component that can't take a colour parameter for this: `RhaydusButton`'s `OUTLINED` style hardcodes its border and label to the theme's `outline`/`onSurface` roles, so the idle "Start reading session" state wraps it in a scoped `MaterialTheme(colorScheme = MaterialTheme.colorScheme.copy(outline = …, onSurface = …))` override (shapes/typography still inherited) so the border stays visible against the image regardless of theme — the filled pill and the active-session tonal pill need no such override, since both carry their own solid fill. There is no split-button/dropdown here — mark-as-read lives only in the Update-progress sheet (below). Reach for this anatomy only for Reading's single featured book; do not reuse the backdrop treatment for a general "hero card" without updating this entry.
- **Reading secondary row.** The "also reading" rows beneath the featured card: a flat, hairline-`outlineVariant`-topped row (drawn on every row including the first, so a run reads as one continuous list — the same idea as the Settings menu row's hairline, above) rather than its own card, 24dp horizontal / 18dp vertical padding. Anatomy: a 54dp cover, a text column (series/mutation-failed line, `titleMedium` title — Roman, not italic; a list-item title stays on the app's Roman convention for card/list titles, §2.2, even though the redline mockup sets it in italic — the italic reading is reserved for headline/body/stat roles), author, an inline compact `DeadlineRow` (calendar + status word only, same labels as the hero) when a deadline is set, and a slim wavy progress line (`LinearWavyProgressIndicator`, `weight(1f)`) with a trailing `NN%`. Reach for this row anatomy for any future Reading-style secondary list; the section opener above it ("Also between your fingers") is the two-tier eyebrow ("N more in motion") + `headlineSmall` headline pattern (§3.2, deliberately stepped down from the redline spec's full `display` role, which read as oversized for a body-section opener), not the single-line `SectionLabel`.
- **Drag-to-reorder custom list (grid variant — ORDER, ranked-gated).** The custom-list counterpart to the MANUAL shelf variant above, with two consequential differences. **(1) Server-owned positions:** the order lives on Hardcover's `list_books.position` column, not in a local-only table. **(2) Gated on the list's `ranked` flag:** the Arrange sheet's sort chips expose `ORDER` only when `lists.ranked = true`. Unranked custom lists show the four in-memory sorts (Title / Author / Page count / Release date) as chips, followed by a "✶ Make this list ordered" text affordance (`bodyMedium` tinted `primary`) that flips `ranked` to true via the `UpdateList` mutation. The flip is optimistic: the local cache toggles immediately, the tab's stored sort auto-switches to `ORDER` so the user lands directly on the reorderable grid, and the server call follows; on failure both are rolled back and a snackbar surfaces ("Couldn't enable manual ordering for "…" — try again."). Like MANUAL, `ORDER` is display-only until the user enters **Rearrange mode** from the masthead control line's "Reorder" hint chip (or the Arrange sheet's "✶ Make this list ordered" affordance, which flips `ranked` and auto-switches the sort to `ORDER` in one tap); drag anatomy and haptics then match the MANUAL variant (overlay handle, `lift` on grab, `drop` on release, drag-only with tap-to-open suppressed while the handle is live). Persistence is **range-scoped**, not prefix-scoped: on drop, only positions `[minTouchedIndex, maxTouchedIndex]` are rewritten (clear-then-set in a single GraphQL mutation that mirrors Hardcover web's two-step `update_list_books` + `update_list_books_many` pattern). Books outside the range keep their existing positions, so a swap of two adjacent middle items only touches those two slots on the server, never the rest of the list — matching the web client's semantics so two devices editing the same list don't fight each other. On server failure the optimistic local write stands and the next list refresh reconciles. Reach for this pattern wherever a server-owned ordered collection needs in-place reorder.
- **Profile screen.** Profile's "reading life" reading top-to-bottom: the reader's portrait/name/bio header, "Reading atlas" (the hero pages-read card + volumes/avg-rating/streak tiles), a **share entry row** (a `surfaceContainer` card — primary glyph tile, two-line prompt, trailing chevron — opening the share sheet below), "The shape of the years" (the peak-annotated bar-column chart, §4, behind a Books/Pages `SingleChoiceSegmentedButtonRow` toggle that is pure composable-local state, not a TOAD action), "The genres you read most" (the genre proportion stack, §4), "How you score" (the half-star ratings histogram, §4, under a headline/caption pair chosen from the reader's classified rating band — never from the bare average), "Recently loved" (up to three real 4★+ reads, cover + title + author + `rating★ · month`), and the demoted **account foot** (a tonal Log out button over a quiet "— · —" colophon). The four new sections (year chart, genre stack, ratings, recently loved) each open with the standard eyebrow + italic headline pair (§3.2); the reader header and "Reading atlas" keep their pre-existing eyebrow-only `SectionLabel` (no headline) as a deliberate carry-over from before the redesign, not a second register. The hero pages card remains the screen's one primary-filled surface (§2.1).
- **Profile share sheet.** The reading-life share sheet (`AdaptiveModalSheet`, §3.5): a mini accent-bar + eyebrow ("SHARE YOUR YEAR") + a prose line, then a scaled, non-interactive preview of the exportable **reading-life share card** (§4 Share card) centred in the sheet, then an actions row — a primary "Save image" pill (`RhaydusButton`, flex) beside a circular `surfaceContainerHigh` share button (`DesktopTooltip`-wrapped, since it is icon-only) — both driving the foundation `ShareCardCapture` seam (`capture.saveToGallery` / `capture.share`) exactly as the book-detail share sheet does. Opened from two places that must stay in sync: the top bar's share glyph action and the share entry row card; both simply flip the same composable-local sheet-visibility boolean, no TOAD action or event.
- **Log-out confirm sheet.** A destructive-confirm `AdaptiveModalSheet` (§3.5) that gates the account foot's Log out button — Profile's Log out is demoted to plumbing (§4 Settings menu row is the sibling convention: navigational chrome, never a headline action), so it never fires directly. Anatomy: an italic `headlineMedium` "Leaving so soon?" over a short prose line, a primary `RhaydusButton` "Log out" pill that dispatches the existing log-out action and then dismisses, and a text "Stay" affordance that just dismisses. No new TOAD state — the sheet's own visibility is composable-local, and "Log out" wires straight through to the screen's existing log-out action.
- **Title-page sheet (Create list).** A centred, single-field modal composition for a surface so small that the usual eyebrow-plus-headline sheet header would outweigh its own content — currently the create-list sheet, "List Creation 1a." Anatomy, top to bottom, all centred: a bar-less `eyebrow` ("A NEW LIST") — the deliberate §2.3 exception, since the rule below already opens the region; a full-width hairline broken in the middle by a small `quoteGlyph` at the `primary` @ 0.28 tint exception (§2.2); a borderless, label-less name hero at display scale, carrying the platform text cursor tinted `primary` and a real-title placeholder (not an instructional label — the placeholder shows the reader what a good entry looks like, e.g. "Summer reads" rather than "List name"); an inset (not full-bleed) hairline divider; the privacy prose toggle sentence (the "Prose toggle" pattern below); and a single full-width filled `RhaydusButton` pill as the only primary action. There is no separate headline — the name field itself carries that weight, which is what makes this composition an exception rather than a shorter version of the canonical header (§3.5). Reach for this pattern only when a sheet's entire job is naming or capturing one short piece of text as an act of authorship; a sheet with more than one field, or a field alongside settings/options, wants the canonical `EditorialSectionHeader` opening instead.

  The hero is **sized to its own characters**: it holds one line and steps its type size down to fit the width available, rather than wrapping or clipping. Three consequences are load-bearing, not incidental. The placeholder must sit *beside* the input rather than stacked behind it — a centred placeholder overlaid on a centred empty field puts the caret in the middle of the placeholder word. It must share the hero's fitted size and stay single-line, or it wraps to a height the single-line field does not have and the caret drifts off the text entirely. And the caret must sit at the placeholder's **leading** edge, not its trailing one: the placeholder is a hint the first keystroke replaces wholesale, so a caret after it promises that typing appends to the example. A caret anywhere but where the next character will land is the tell that this pattern has been rebuilt as a plain centred text field with a placeholder layered over it.
- **Prose toggle.** A sentence rendered as normal running prose where one or two words are themselves the control — tapping a word selects it, in place, with no separate widget beside the sentence. Anatomy: the chosen word renders in `primary`, a heavier weight, and a thin (2dp) `primary` underline; every other candidate word renders demoted (`onSurfaceVariant` at reduced alpha) with a **transparent** underline of the same thickness reserved beneath it, so choosing a different word never reflows the sentence. Each word's own tap target is expanded via `Modifier.minimumInteractiveComponentSize()` rather than by enlarging the glyph, so a short word stays typographically correct while still meeting the 48dp minimum. Reach for this instead of a `Switch` or segmented control when the setting is genuinely binary, reads naturally as a sentence, and does not need a caption to explain itself — the sentence already says what it means (the create-list sheet's "This shelf is *public* / *private*." is the reference use). It is not yet promoted to a shared component (currently feature-local to create-list, `feature/lists`); promote it to `core/presentation/component` once a second surface needs the same "sentence you tap to flip" control, the way the Profile chart family (§4) waits for its second consumer.
